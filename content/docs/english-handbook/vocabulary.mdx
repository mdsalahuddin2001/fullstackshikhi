## 01 · How to use this companion
### Learn the expression, not only the translation
“Suggest” means প্রস্তাব দেওয়া, but meaning alone does not tell you how to build the sentence. Learn “suggest doing something”: “I suggest leaving early.” Do not copy the pattern of “want to leave” into “suggest to leave.”

Each card contains: **target expression → Bangla meaning → pattern or context → example**. Treat the meaning as the sense used in that example. A word such as charge, point, or mean can have other meanings elsewhere.

### Five entries, five personal sentences
1. Read the English expression and its Bangla meaning.
2. Say the example aloud and notice the word partners.
3. Cover the example and use the expression in your own sentence.
4. Explain the situation in which you would say your sentence.
5. Review tomorrow and again after 3, 7, and 14 days. Adjust the schedule if needed.

**Example:** “available” → সময় আছে এমন → “Are you available on Friday?” → “I'm available after work, but not before six.”

### Test active use
Recognition: “I understand it when I see it.” Recall: “I can produce it from a situation.” Flexible use: “I can change the person, time, or purpose.” Aim for flexible use. Do not count an entry as mastered merely because its Bangla meaning looks familiar.

### Read patterns carefully
“+ -ing” means an -ing form follows: avoid rushing. “Base verb” means the dictionary form: can help. “Someone/something” are slots to replace: explain the issue to me. They are not words you must say in every sentence.

For separable phrasal verbs, a pronoun often goes in the middle: turn it off, pick me up, sort it out. For look after or look into, it stays after the full expression: look after her, look into it.

### Where to find things
Themes 1-20 are on pages 2-21. Confusing expressions are on pages 22-23. Context practice is on pages 24-25. Answers are on page 26. The alphabetical index starts on page 27.

The 240 targets include single words, phrasal verbs, and conversation phrases. The verb list in Book 1 gives additional forms and Bangla meanings for common verbs.

## Theme 1 · Starting and maintaining conversations

**introduce** · পরিচয় করিয়ে দেওয়া
Pattern: introduce yourself / someone to someone
Example: Let me introduce you to my colleague.

**mention** · উল্লেখ করা
Pattern: mention something; no “about” needed
Example: You mentioned a new course. How is it going?

**clarify** · পরিষ্কার করে বলা
Pattern: clarify a point / what you mean
Example: Could you clarify what you mean by “ready”?

**repeat** · আবার বলা বা করা
Pattern: repeat a word / sentence
Example: Could you repeat the last part, please?

**mean** · অর্থ বোঝানো / বোঝাতে চাওয়া
Pattern: What do you mean by…?
Example: What do you mean by “flexible”?

**refer to** · উল্লেখ করা / বোঝানো
Pattern: refer to a person / topic
Example: Are you referring to yesterday's meeting?

**interrupt** · কথার মাঝে বাধা দেওয়া
Pattern: interrupt someone
Example: Sorry to interrupt, but I have a question.

**continue** · চালিয়ে যাওয়া
Pattern: continue doing / to do something
Example: Please continue. I'm listening.

**respond** · উত্তর দেওয়া / প্রতিক্রিয়া জানানো
Pattern: respond to a question / message
Example: I'll respond to your message this evening.

**explain** · ব্যাখ্যা করা
Pattern: explain something to someone
Example: Could you explain the difference to me?

**summarize** · সংক্ষেপে বলা
Pattern: summarize the main points
Example: Let me summarize what we agreed on.

**by the way** · প্রসঙ্গক্রমে / কথায় কথায়
Pattern: introduce a related or new topic
Example: By the way, are you free tomorrow?

## Theme 5 · Time, frequency, and progress

**recently** · সম্প্রতি
Pattern: a recent past event / situation
Example: I've recently started a new course.

**currently** · বর্তমানে
Pattern: a situation happening now
Example: I'm currently working from home.

**eventually** · কিছু সময় বা বাধার পর অবশেষে
Pattern: an outcome after delay or effort
Example: We eventually found the right building.

**meanwhile** · এদিকে / একই সময়ে
Pattern: a parallel activity
Example: I'll cook dinner. Meanwhile, you can set the table.

**already** · ইতিমধ্যে
Pattern: earlier than now or expected
Example: I've already sent the message.

**yet** · এখনও / ইতিমধ্যে কি না
Pattern: mainly questions and negatives
Example: Have you finished yet? I haven't finished yet.

**still** · এখনও
Pattern: a situation that continues
Example: I'm still waiting for a reply.

**no longer** · আর নয় / এখন আর করে না
Pattern: a situation that has ended
Example: I no longer work at that office.

**occasionally** · মাঝে মাঝে
Pattern: something happens sometimes
Example: We occasionally have lunch together.

**regularly** · নিয়মিতভাবে
Pattern: a repeated habit
Example: I review my notes regularly.

**in advance** · আগে থেকে
Pattern: do something before it is needed
Example: Please book your seat in advance.

**on time** · নির্ধারিত সময়ে
Pattern: punctual; compare “in time”
Example: The train arrived on time. We got there in time to catch it.

## 22 · Similar words, different jobs
| Pair | Difference | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| say / tell | Say something; tell someone something. | She said hello. She told me the news. |
| speak / talk | Both can describe conversation; speak also names language ability. | I speak Bangla. Let's talk about the plan. |
| hear / listen | Hear = শুনতে পাওয়া; listen = মন দিয়ে শোনা. | I heard a noise. Listen to the instructions. |
| look / see / watch | Look = তাকানো; see = দেখতে পাওয়া; watch = মনোযোগ দিয়ে দেখা. | Look at the photo. I can see you. We watched a film. |
| borrow / lend | Borrow = ধার নেওয়া; lend = ধার দেওয়া. | Can I borrow your pen? Could you lend me your pen? |
| bring / take | Bring often moves toward the speaker or reference point; take moves elsewhere. | Bring your notes to our meeting. Take this form to the office. |
| job / work | Job is countable; work is usually uncountable when meaning employment or tasks. | I have a new job. I have a lot of work today. |
| advice / advise | Advice is a noun; advise is a verb. | Thanks for the advice. What do you advise me to do? |
| affect / effect | Affect is usually a verb; effect is usually a noun in everyday use. | Noise affects my sleep. The change had a positive effect. |
| remember / remind | Remember = মনে থাকা; remind = মনে করিয়ে দেওয়া. | I remember the date. Please remind me tomorrow. |
| bored / boring | Bored describes how someone feels; boring describes what causes that feeling. | I felt bored. The talk was boring. |
| interested / interesting | Interested describes the person's response; interesting describes the thing. | I'm interested in history. The story is interesting. |

### Practise both sides
Say two true sentences with each pair. Then explain the difference in Bangla. For example: “Can I borrow your book?” means আমি কি আপনার বইটা ধার নিতে পারি? “Can you lend me your book?” asks the owner to give it temporarily. Both can achieve the same practical request, but the grammatical perspective changes.

**Tone note:** Calling a person boring can sound insulting. Describe the specific activity or explain your preference when that is what you mean.

## 23 · Small differences that change the meaning
| Expressions | Difference and examples |
| --- | --- |
| on time / in time | On time = নির্ধারিত সময়ে: “The class started on time.” In time = দেরি হওয়ার আগে: “I arrived in time to get a seat.” |
| by / until | By sets the latest completion time: “Send it by Friday.” Until describes continuation: “I'll stay until Friday.” |
| since / for | Since + starting point: “since 2022.” For + duration: “for four years.” |
| used to / be used to / get used to | Past habit: “I used to work nights.” Familiar state: “I'm used to working nights.” Becoming familiar: “I'm getting used to working nights.” |
| stop doing / stop to do | “I stopped checking my phone” = I quit that action. “I stopped to check my phone” = I paused another activity so I could check it. |
| mustn't / don't have to | “You mustn't enter” = entry is prohibited. “You don't have to enter” = entry is not required. |
| few / a few | “I have few options” emphasizes scarcity. “I have a few options” emphasizes that some exist. Both take plural countable nouns. |
| little / a little | “I have little time” emphasizes shortage. “I have a little time” means some time is available. Both take uncountable nouns. |
| alone / lonely | Alone = একা থাকা. Lonely = একাকিত্ব অনুভব করা. “I was alone, but I didn't feel lonely.” |
| actually / currently | Actually = আসলে; currently = বর্তমানে. “Actually, I live in Sylhet.” “I'm currently visiting Dhaka.” |
| especially / specially | Especially often means particularly: “I enjoy reading, especially stories.” Specially often means for a particular purpose: “This bag was specially made for the equipment.” |
| fluent / accurate | Fluent emphasizes ease and flow; accurate emphasizes correctness. You can be stronger in one than the other. Practise both. |

### Two polite-response traps
“Would you mind opening the door?” → “Not at all” or “Of course, I'll open it.” A bare “yes” can imply that you do mind. A clear action response avoids confusion.

“I'm afraid I can't come” usually introduces unwelcome news politely. It does not necessarily mean the speaker feels fear. Likewise, “I see your point” acknowledges understanding; it does not necessarily mean agreement.
