01 · How to use this companion

Learn the expression, not only the translation

“Suggest” means প্রস্তাব দেওয়া, but meaning alone does not tell you how to build the sentence. Learn “suggest doing something”: “I suggest leaving early.” Do not copy the pattern of “want to leave” into “suggest to leave.”

Each card contains: target expression → Bangla meaning → pattern or context → example. Treat the meaning as the sense used in that example. A word such as charge, point, or mean can have other meanings elsewhere.

Five entries, five personal sentences

1. Read the English expression and its Bangla meaning.

2. Say the example aloud and notice the word partners.

3. Cover the example and use the expression in your own sentence.

4. Explain the situation in which you would say your sentence.

5. Review tomorrow and again after 3, 7, and 14 days. Adjust the schedule if needed.

Example: “available” → সময় আছে এমন → “Are you available on Friday?” → “I'm available after work, but not before six.”

Test active use

Recognition: “I understand it when I see it.” Recall: “I can produce it from a situation.” Flexible use: “I can change the person, time, or purpose.” Aim for flexible use. Do not count an entry as mastered merely because its Bangla meaning looks familiar.

Read patterns carefully

“+ -ing” means an -ing form follows: avoid rushing. “Base verb” means the dictionary form: can help. “Someone/something” are slots to replace: explain the issue to me. They are not words you must say in every sentence.

For separable phrasal verbs, a pronoun often goes in the middle: turn it off, pick me up, sort it out. For look after or look into, it stays after the full expression: look after her, look into it.

Where to find things

Themes 1-20 are on pages 2-21. Confusing expressions are on pages 22-23. Context practice is on pages 24-25. Answers are on page 26. The alphabetical index starts on page 27.

The 240 targets include single words, phrasal verbs, and conversation phrases. The verb list in Book 1 gives additional forms and Bangla meanings for common verbs.

22 · Similar words, different jobs

PairDifferenceExamples
say / tellSay something; tell someone something.She said hello. She told me the news.
speak / talkBoth can describe conversation; speak also names language ability.I speak Bangla. Let's talk about the plan.
hear / listenHear = শুনতে পাওয়া; listen = মন দিয়ে শোনা.I heard a noise. Listen to the instructions.
look / see / watchLook = তাকানো; see = দেখতে পাওয়া; watch = মনোযোগ দিয়ে দেখা.Look at the photo. I can see you. We watched a film.
borrow / lendBorrow = ধার নেওয়া; lend = ধার দেওয়া.Can I borrow your pen? Could you lend me your pen?
bring / takeBring often moves toward the speaker or reference point; take moves elsewhere.Bring your notes to our meeting. Take this form to the office.
job / workJob is countable; work is usually uncountable when meaning employment or tasks.I have a new job. I have a lot of work today.
advice / adviseAdvice is a noun; advise is a verb.Thanks for the advice. What do you advise me to do?
affect / effectAffect is usually a verb; effect is usually a noun in everyday use.Noise affects my sleep. The change had a positive effect.
remember / remindRemember = মনে থাকা; remind = মনে করিয়ে দেওয়া.I remember the date. Please remind me tomorrow.
bored / boringBored describes how someone feels; boring describes what causes that feeling.I felt bored. The talk was boring.
interested / interestingInterested describes the person's response; interesting describes the thing.I'm interested in history. The story is interesting.

Practise both sides

Say two true sentences with each pair. Then explain the difference in Bangla. For example: “Can I borrow your book?” means আমি কি আপনার বইটা ধার নিতে পারি? “Can you lend me your book?” asks the owner to give it temporarily. Both can achieve the same practical request, but the grammatical perspective changes.

Tone note: Calling a person boring can sound insulting. Describe the specific activity or explain your preference when that is what you mean.

23 · Small differences that change the meaning

ExpressionsDifference and examples
on time / in timeOn time = নির্ধারিত সময়ে: “The class started on time.” In time = দেরি হওয়ার আগে: “I arrived in time to get a seat.”
by / untilBy sets the latest completion time: “Send it by Friday.” Until describes continuation: “I'll stay until Friday.”
since / forSince + starting point: “since 2022.” For + duration: “for four years.”
used to / be used to / get used toPast habit: “I used to work nights.” Familiar state: “I'm used to working nights.” Becoming familiar: “I'm getting used to working nights.”
stop doing / stop to do“I stopped checking my phone” = I quit that action. “I stopped to check my phone” = I paused another activity so I could check it.
mustn't / don't have to“You mustn't enter” = entry is prohibited. “You don't have to enter” = entry is not required.
few / a few“I have few options” emphasizes scarcity. “I have a few options” emphasizes that some exist. Both take plural countable nouns.
little / a little“I have little time” emphasizes shortage. “I have a little time” means some time is available. Both take uncountable nouns.
alone / lonelyAlone = একা থাকা. Lonely = একাকিত্ব অনুভব করা. “I was alone, but I didn't feel lonely.”
actually / currentlyActually = আসলে; currently = বর্তমানে. “Actually, I live in Sylhet.” “I'm currently visiting Dhaka.”
especially / speciallyEspecially often means particularly: “I enjoy reading, especially stories.” Specially often means for a particular purpose: “This bag was specially made for the equipment.”
fluent / accurateFluent emphasizes ease and flow; accurate emphasizes correctness. You can be stronger in one than the other. Practise both.

Two polite-response traps

“Would you mind opening the door?” → “Not at all” or “Of course, I'll open it.” A bare “yes” can imply that you do mind. A clear action response avoids confusion.

“I'm afraid I can't come” usually introduces unwelcome news politely. It does not necessarily mean the speaker feels fear. Likewise, “I see your point” acknowledges understanding; it does not necessarily mean agreement.

1. clarify
It asks for a clearer explanation.

2. explain
Use explain something to someone.

3. assumed
An assumption is not verified.

4. figure out
You need to work out the method.

5. leaving
Suggest can take an -ing form.

6. to wait
Allow someone to do something.

7. reschedule
You are assigning a new time.

8. remind
Remind someone to do something.

9. yet
Yet fits this negative sentence.

10. in advance
Book before the ticket is needed.

11. for
Responsible for + noun or -ing.

12. feedback
Feedback is normally uncountable.

13. relieved
Relieved describes reduced worry.

14. for
Grateful for something.

15. reading
Worth can take an -ing form.

16. on
Depend on is the usual combination.

17. out of
There is no milk left.

18. resolved
Resolve means settle a problem.

19. along with
This describes a good relationship.

20. in touch
Keep in touch means maintain contact.

21. receipt
A receipt records a purchase.

22. for
Exchange something for something.

23. me up
A pronoun goes between pick and up.

24. off
Get off a bus or train.

25. exhausted
Exhausted means very tired.

26. down on
Cut down on means reduce.

27. attachment
An attachment is a linked email file.

28. it up
A pronoun goes between back and up.

29. speaking
Get used to takes a noun or -ing form.

30. on
Focus on is the usual combination.

31. used to
This contrasts a past habit with now.

32. walking
End up can take an -ing form.

33. into
Look into means investigate.

34. it off
A pronoun goes in the middle.

35. at
Have a look at something.

36. reading
Spend time doing something.

37. closing
Would you mind takes an -ing form.

38. works
That works for me accepts an arrangement.

39. taking
Instead of takes a noun or -ing form.

40. have
Use present simple in this future-condition pattern.
